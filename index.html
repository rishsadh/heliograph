Institute of Solar Memory one star, observed without interruption

Every clear morning since the third of April, 1874, this institute has made one photograph of the sun. Nineteen keepers. Fifty-four thousand plates. A single unbroken sentence about a star that does not know we are writing it.

§ 04The Field

Nothing here
is solid.

What looks like a surface is a lid of boiling gas one thousand kilometres thick, held in shape by a magnetic field that turns itself inside out every eleven years. The loops you see are not drawn. They are traced, one particle at a time, along the field as we last measured it.
